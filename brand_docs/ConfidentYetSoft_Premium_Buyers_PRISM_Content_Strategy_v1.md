# AUDIT: What the Old Funnel Got Wrong

The old funnel was a competent mass-market funnel. It was well-structured for a $297–$997 offer sold to a broad audience. The Personalized PCOS Protocol is a $3,000 offer sold to a specific, high-caliber buyer. The funnel was not built for her.

The problems are structural, not cosmetic. Here is each one:

### Problem 1: The Ideal Client Was Described as Broken

**OLD:** "Blames herself for not having enough willpower... overthinking, procrastinating, staying hyper-independent." She is described by what she cannot do.

**WHY THIS FAILS:** Your $3,000 buyer does not identify as someone who lacks willpower or can't figure things out. She identifies as capable, ambitious, and already trying harder than most. Describing her as broken triggers an identity mismatch — she does not see herself in the content and disengages.

**THE FIX:** Every client description in content and in this funnel describes her identity first — ambitious, strategic, future-oriented — and her challenge second. The challenge is the natural complexity of doing ambitious things with an undertreated hormonal condition. Not a personal failing.

### Problem 2: The Funnel Was Built for the Awareness Stage

**OLD:** The funnel leaned almost entirely on awareness content — myth-busting, symptom education, willpower reframes. The content assumed she didn't know what PCOS was or that her hormones were involved.

**WHY THIS FAILS:** Your actual buyer is not in the awareness stage. She already knows she has PCOS. She has already been to the doctor. She has already tried things. She is in the decision stage — she is evaluating whether your specific methodology is the right next move for her specifically.

**THE FIX:** The content mix shifts to 30% reframe/awareness content and 70% differentiation, authority, and decision content. She does not need to be educated on PCOS. She needs to understand why your protocol is the only one that addresses her specific hormonal picture.

### Problem 3: The CTAs Were Engagement CTAs, Not Buyer CTAs

**OLD:** "Comment SOFT LIFE if this feels like you." / "Save for when you forget you're not lazy." / "DM RESET to get the free guide."

**WHY THIS FAILS:** These CTAs are optimized for engagement metrics — saves, comments, warm feelings. They attract women who enjoy the content. They do not convert premium buyers. A $3,000 buyer does not DM "SOFT LIFE." She DMs when she has made a decision.

**THE FIX:** CTAs speak to action-readiness, not emotional resonance. "DM CYSTER if you're ready to stop guessing and get the protocol built around your hormonal picture." Every CTA assumes she is already considering — it gives her a clear next step, not a soft way to signal interest.

### Problem 4: Social Proof Was Posted Without a Methodology Frame

**OLD:** Client before/afters and wins were posted as emotional proof — "from PCOS belly to soft, glowing, confident."

**WHY THIS FAILS:** Emotional social proof builds relatability. It does not build premium trust. A $3,000 buyer needs to understand the mechanism behind the result — not just that it happened, but why, and whether the same mechanism applies to her.

**THE FIX:** Every result is framed through the methodology. "After completing the Personalized PCOS Protocol, Jade's HbA1c dropped, her cycle regulated in 30 days, and she lost 26 lbs in 5 months — working 12-hour nursing shifts. Here is the specific hormonal driver we addressed first."

### Problem 5: "Soft Life" Was Positioned as a Lifestyle Aesthetic

**OLD:** The funnel led with candles, cozy bedrooms, silk robes, and "your soft era."

**WHY THIS FAILS:** This positions the brand as a wellness lifestyle account, not a specialist health authority. Lifestyle content attracts lifestyle followers. It does not attract women making a $3,000 health investment. A premium buyer does not buy your program because your feed is aesthetically calming.

**THE FIX:** "Success Through Softness" is repositioned as a strategic performance principle — the scientific case that nervous system regulation is a clinical prerequisite for hormonal balance. Softness is not an aesthetic. It is a mechanism.

### Problem 6: The Funnel Had No Premium Buyer Filter

**OLD:** The funnel was built to attract as many women as possible and convince them over time. There was no structural mechanism to pre-qualify or pre-disqualify.

**WHY THIS FAILS:** Without a filter, you attract a broad audience including students, women who cannot afford the program, and women who want free content indefinitely. This dilutes your community quality and creates a sales process where you are convincing reluctant buyers — which is the wrong sales conversation for a $3,000 offer.

**THE FIX:** The new funnel is built with identity-based filtering in every stage. Content is written to be instantly recognizable by the right buyer and quietly irrelevant to the wrong one. The right 500 women in your audience are worth more than 50,000 wrong ones.

# Why We Are Replacing the 4-Stage Funnel with PRISM

The standard 4-stage marketing funnel (Awareness, Consideration, Conversion, Retention) was designed for consumer goods and low-ticket services. It assumes a linear buyer who starts uninformed and moves rationally toward purchase. It was not designed for high-ticket health offers sold to high-agency buyers.

High-ticket buyers do not move linearly through awareness to conversion. They move through a trust architecture. Research on high-ticket buyer behavior (Donata White buyer psychology framework, Blue Ocean Strategy buyer mapping) identifies five decision triggers that must be activated before a premium buyer commits:

- Category recognition — "this is a different class of solution"
- Identity congruence — "this provider understands exactly who I am"
- Methodology confidence — "I understand why this will work when other things haven't"
- Proof certainty — "I have seen this work for women exactly like me"
- Urgency from internal logic — "waiting is more costly than investing"

The PRISM framework is built around these five decision triggers. Each stage produces one of them. By the time a qualified buyer has moved through all five stages, she is not being sold — she is executing a decision she has already made.

Each stage is explained in detail in the following pages, including the marketing psychology behind it, specific content ideas, and the CTA that activates the next stage.

| Stage | Name | Decision Trigger Activated | Content Ratio |
|-------|------|---------------------------|---------------|
| P | Pattern Interrupt | Category recognition | 15% |
| R | Reframe | Category recognition (deeper) | 20% |
| I | Identity Mirror | Identity congruence | 25% |
| S | System Authority | Methodology confidence + proof certainty | 25% |
| M | Momentum | Internal urgency + action | 15% |

# STAGE 1

# PATTERN INTERRUPT

Stop the scroll. Create category recognition.

### What This Stage Does

Pattern Interrupt content exists for one purpose: to make an already-scrolling woman stop and think "that is not what I expected to hear." It does not educate. It disrupts. It makes her realize the frame she has been using to understand her problem is incomplete — and that there is a different, more accurate frame available.

### The Marketing Psychology: Cognitive Disruption and Selective Attention

The human brain filters 99% of incoming information as irrelevant. What breaks through the filter is either: (1) something emotionally threatening, or (2) something that contradicts an existing belief with enough authority to be credible rather than dismissed.

This is why the old "You're not lazy, it's your hormones" content underperformed for premium buyers. It confirmed what she already knew. It produced comfort, not disruption. A buyer who is comfortable does not take action.

The pattern interrupt for your premium buyer is not "your hormones are the problem" — she already knows that. It is "you have been addressing the wrong hormonal driver" or "the protocol you received was designed for a different body than yours" — something that creates the productive discomfort of realizing her current frame is incomplete.

### What This Content Looks Like

**Format: Bold contradiction statements**

- "Your personal trainer cannot fix your PCOS belly. Here's why that's not her fault."
- "Losing weight will not regulate your period. The order of operations is backwards."
- "Every doctor who told you to just lose weight was solving the wrong equation."
- "You've been consistent. You've been disciplined. The protocol was wrong, not you."

**Format: Reframe of something she has invested in**

- "What a $200/month personal trainer can and cannot do for a woman with PCOS — specifically."
- "The nutritionist gave you a Mediterranean diet plan. Your insulin resistance does not respond to the Mediterranean diet the way a metabolically healthy body does."
- "Ozempic suppressed your appetite. It did not touch your androgen levels, your cortisol, or your cycle. Here's what that means for where you are now."

**Format: The "Category of One" opening**

- "There are PCOS accounts. There are weight loss coaches. There is one program that treats PCOS as a hormonal condition first and a weight problem second. This is what that looks like."

**Specific Content Ideas:**

- Reel: "The reason your scale won't move has nothing to do with calories. As a clinical exercise physiologist who works with PCOS specifically, here is the first thing I assess."
- Reel: "I spent 3 years praising myself for how hard I worked. My cortisol was through the roof, my period had disappeared, and I thought I was doing everything right. Here's what I missed."
- Carousel: "Why everything you've tried for PCOS belly fat has a structural reason it didn't work — and it's not about consistency."
- B-roll text overlay: "Girls under 30 with PCOS — the sooner you understand that weight loss cannot come before hormone regulation, the sooner everything changes."
- Talking head: "I have a Masters of Public Health and I am telling you: the standard advice women with PCOS receive is built for a metabolically average body."

### CTA for This Stage

CTA: "If this reframes something you've been told, save this and follow for the hormone-first approach. When you're ready for the personalized protocol, DM CYSTER."

### What NOT to Do in This Stage

- Do not use empathy hooks ("I know how exhausting it is") — this signals mass-market, not specialist
- Do not open with a symptom list she already knows she has — this confirms, not disrupts
- Do not use the "breaking news" template or viral trend formats — these signal tips account, not authority

# STAGE 2

# REFRAME

Change how she understands the problem — using your framework.

### What This Stage Does

Reframe content takes the disruption from Stage 1 and replaces the old mental model with your proprietary one. She stopped scrolling because something felt off. Reframe content gives her the correct framework to replace what wasn't working.

### The Marketing Psychology: Schema Replacement and Language Lock-In

From the Donata White framework: when a buyer adopts your language to describe their problem, cognitive lock-in occurs. She can no longer articulate her problem without using your vocabulary. This is the most powerful pre-sell mechanism available in content marketing — more powerful than any testimonial or before-and-after.

This is why the reframe must be specific and proprietary — not generic hormonal education. "Cortisol causes weight gain" is information. "Your cortisol pattern has been blocking insulin sensitivity since before you started the last program, which is why the scale didn't move" is a diagnosis. Diagnoses create lock-in. Information creates saves.

The goal: she should finish every Reframe piece thinking in your language. "My problem is not belly fat — it's that my insulin resistance is untreated and my cortisol is dysregulated. I need a hormone-first protocol."

### What This Content Looks Like

**Reframe the diagnosis:**

- Reel: "PCOS is not a weight problem. it is a hormonal condition that creates a weight symptom. every program designed to treat the symptom before the condition will produce temporary results at best."
- Carousel: "The 3-step order of operations your PCOS protocol needs to follow — and why most programs get it backwards."
- Reel: "Insulin resistance does not respond to caloric deficit the way a metabolically healthy body does. Here is the specific mechanism — and the specific intervention that addresses it."

**Reframe the solution category:**

- Reel: "The difference between a weight loss program and a hormone-first protocol is not the food or the workouts. It's the starting point. Here's what a hormone-first assessment looks at that a standard intake misses."
- Carousel: "Why culturally intelligent nutrition is not a 'nice to have' for PCOS — it's a clinical requirement. Your hormonal response to food is shaped by your genetics, your heritage, and your stress pattern. A generic meal plan does not account for any of those."
- Talking head: "Every program I tried gave me a plan that worked for someone. Not for me. My body, my hormones, my cultural background, my cortisol load — none of it was part of the equation. That's what the Personalized PCOS Protocol changes."

**Reframe the hustle-health connection:**

- Reel: "Your ambition is not the problem. The cortisol load that comes with 60-hour weeks and skipped meals and 5am workouts on 5 hours of sleep is clinically documented to worsen PCOS. The protocol does not ask you to slow down. It asks you to be smarter about the biology."
- B-roll: "The high-achiever habits that are clinically documented to worsen PCOS symptoms — and what to replace them with inside the 10-Hour Hormone Optimization Window."

### CTA for This Stage

CTA: "Follow for the hormone-first approach to PCOS that no one else is teaching. When you're ready for the personalized protocol, DM CYSTER."

# STAGE 3

# IDENTITY MIRROR

Make her feel precisely seen as who she is — not what she's navigating.

### What This Stage Does

Identity Mirror content creates the "she is talking directly to me" experience — not through emotional empathy, but through hyper-specific description of her professional context, her investment history, and her ambitions. She is not seen because you tell her you see her. She is seen because the description is accurate enough that it could only have been written by someone who actually works with women like her.

### The Marketing Psychology: Self-Concept Congruence

Consumer psychology research (Sirgy, 1982; extended by multiple luxury goods studies) shows that buyers purchase from brands whose identity is congruent with their own self-concept. For premium health offers, this means the content must reflect the buyer's professional identity, her decision-making style, and her values — not just her symptoms.

"For the woman who has already tried everything the mainstream recommends and knows the root cause is somewhere they haven't looked yet" is an identity mirror. "For women with PCOS who are tired of struggling" is not.

Premium buyers do not buy solutions to their problems. They buy confirmation that a provider sees them clearly enough to be trusted with their health.

### What This Content Looks Like

**Mirror her professional identity:**

- Reel: "This is for the woman who has a demanding job, a full social life, a health goal she's been serious about for years, and a body that has not responded the way it should have. That is not a motivation problem. That is an untreated hormonal pattern."
- B-roll text: "For the nurse working 12-hour shifts who is serious about her cycle and her weight and does not have time for a program that requires meal prep on Sundays and a full gym schedule. The protocol adapts to your life. Not the other way around."
- Reel: "The lawyer, the corporate director, the entrepreneur who has read every article, tried every supplement, and knows something is structurally wrong. This is what that something is."

**Mirror her investment history:**

- Reel: "If you have spent money on a personal trainer, a nutritionist, a dietitian, birth control to regulate your cycle, supplements, skincare for your acne, and a therapist for the anxiety that comes with all of it — and you still don't have regular periods and a body that feels like yours — the issue was not you. It was the sequence."
- Carousel: "Why everything she tried was the right instinct and the wrong tool." Each tool: what it does well, what it cannot address, what it needs to be paired with for a woman with PCOS.

**Mirror her ambitions, not her problems:**

- Reel: "She is preparing for motherhood. She is building a career. She has a vision for her life and a body that has not been cooperating with it. The Personalized PCOS Protocol does not ask her to put her ambitions on hold. It builds the protocol around them."
- Founder story reel: "I had a 6-figure income, every external marker of success, and a body that had been quietly failing me for 3 years. I understood the biology. I could not figure out why none of it was working. This is the thing I missed."

**Mirror the faith and grief dimension (founder-led):**

- Reel: "I am building this business from my mother's basement, navigating grief after losing my father, working through a faith that has been tested. I am showing up anyway. Not because I have it figured out. Because this work matters and the women I serve deserve consistency."
- Story content: "Grieving out loud, unapologetically. Because silence almost broke me. And because the women I coach deserve to know that the person holding their protocol is a whole person — not a highlight reel."

### CTA for This Stage

CTA: "If you are the woman I just described, the Personalized PCOS Protocol was built specifically for you. DM CYSTER to start the conversation."

# STAGE 4

# SYSTEM AUTHORITY

Make the PPP the only logical solution. No comparison necessary.

### What This Stage Does

System Authority content builds the case for the Personalized PCOS Protocol as a categorically different solution — not better than competitors, but operating in a different category entirely. By the time a buyer has been through Stages 1–3, she is looking for confirmation that the methodology matches the diagnosis she has formed in her own mind.

### The Marketing Psychology: Category of One and Pre-Sold Demand

From the Donata White and Blue Ocean Strategy frameworks: once a buyer has adopted your diagnostic language and feels recognized, she has self-selected into your category. The job of System Authority content is to close that cognitive loop by showing her, in concrete terms, how the methodology works.

The goal is not to attack competitors. It is to make the comparison irrelevant. A buyer who understands the mechanism does not need to compare — she already knows that no other program addresses the root cause the way this one does.

She should leave every System Authority piece thinking: "I now understand why the PPP works when nothing else has. The only question is when I start."

### What This Content Looks Like

**The Coach A vs Coach B mechanism:**

- Carousel: "Coach A has a 12-week PCOS program with meal plans, workouts, and check-ins. Her client asks: how is yours different? Here is the specific mechanism that separates a generic PCOS program from a hormone-first protocol."
- Reel: "The question your program should make her ask is not 'how are you different?' It's 'how soon can I start?' Here is what creates that buyer response."

**Methodology demonstrations:**

- Reel: "Here is what a Personalized PCOS Protocol assessment actually looks at — versus what a standard health coaching intake covers."

- Behind-the-scenes: "This is what PCOS Mentor Morgan's clinical background brings to your protocol that a certified personal trainer cannot."

# STAGE 5

# MOMENTUM

Close the already-warm buyer. No convincing. Just a clear next step.

### What This Stage Does

Momentum content is not about persuasion. By this stage, the qualified buyer has been through Pattern Interrupt, Reframe, Identity Mirror, and System Authority. She understands the mechanism. She recognizes herself in the content. She trusts the methodology. The only thing between her and booking is residual inertia — the gap between "I am convinced" and "I am acting."

### The Marketing Psychology: Certainty Reduction and Commitment Consistency

High-ticket buyers stall at the final step for two reasons: (1) residual uncertainty about whether the result will happen for her specifically, and (2) the mental activation energy required to make the first move.

Certainty is reduced by showing women exactly like her getting results. Not general PCOS women. Nurses working 12-hour shifts. Corporate directors who had tried 4 other programs. Women who had already spent money on the wrong tools before finding the right one.

The Momentum stage does not ask her if she's ready. It assumes she is and gives her a clear, low-friction first step.

### What This Content Looks Like

**Similarity-based social proof:**

- Result reel: "She was a nurse working 12-hour shifts who had tried personal training, a nutritionist, and a weight loss program. Jade lost 26 lbs in 5 months, her period returned pain-free, and she did it while keeping her schedule intact. The mechanism: insulin resistance and cortisol — addressed in the correct sequence."
- Result reel: "Kika's scale had not moved in years. She was consistent. She had tried every recommended approach. The number was not moving because the protocol had not addressed the hormonal driver. After the Personalized PCOS Protocol identified and addressed it, she hit a new low of 140 with a regular 30-day cycle."
- Testimonial clip or screenshot: "My period came today and I didn't even know it was coming. I didn't feel it. I was at work and I realized. I didn't have pain. I didn't have warning." — Jade

**Internal urgency — the cost of waiting:**

- Reel: "Every month you wait is another month of untreated insulin resistance. Another month without a regular cycle. Another month further from the result you've been trying to get for years. The protocol does not get harder when you start sooner."
- Reel: "You have already spent money on this problem. The trainer. The nutritionist. The supplements. The skincare. The second opinion. The Personalized PCOS Protocol addresses the root cause. Not the symptoms. The waiting has a cost."

**Friction removal — what the next step actually looks like:**

- Reel: "Here is exactly what happens when you DM CYSTER. We have a short conversation to understand your hormonal picture. If the protocol is a fit, you start within the week. That's it. No long application. No waiting list. A conversation and a decision."
- Story/post: "We have [X] spots available in the next cohort of the Personalized PCOS Protocol. If you have been following along and you know the mechanism applies to you, this is the week to DM CYSTER."

### CTA for This Stage

# Retention — Converting Clients into Advocates

## The Retention Reframe

The old retention strategy was built around community warmth, sisterhood, and the "soft life lifestyle." These are not wrong. But they are not sufficient for premium client retention.

Premium retention is built on three things: continued measurable results, the identity she has grown into during the protocol, and the community of women at the same level. When those three are present, she does not leave — because leaving would mean leaving the version of herself she became.

### The Marketing Psychology: Identity-Based Loyalty

Research on high-ticket coaching retention shows that clients who have redefined their identity during a program (from "woman managing PCOS" to "woman who has resolved her PCOS hormonal pattern") have 3x higher referral rates and 5x higher repurchase rates than clients who achieved results but did not undergo identity transformation.

The retention strategy is built to produce that identity shift — not just the result.

### Retention Touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Purpose | The Premium Buyer Psychology Behind It |
|------------|---------|----------------------------------------|
| Client results content | Show the methodology working publicly | Validates their investment, creates pride, generates referrals from similar women |
| Case study spotlights | Feature the full arc of a client's result | Identity mirror for prospective clients; identity reinforcement for current clients |
| Masterclasses & Q&As | Fresh clinical education + protocol updates | Signals ongoing value beyond the initial program; premium buyers expect continued development |
| Community content | Behind-the-scenes of client community | Social proof of the standard of client; attracts similar buyers; reinforces belonging |
| Referral strategy | Make sharing the obvious next move | Frame it as advocacy, not promotion: "if someone you know is where you were 4 months ago" |

## How to Generate Referrals from Premium Clients

Premium clients do not refer because you asked them to or because there's a discount attached. They refer because the referral is an act of expertise — they are sharing knowledge, not doing a favor.

- Create a framing moment: "If someone you know has been trying to lose PCOS belly fat or regulate their cycle and nothing has worked, the reason is almost certainly the sequence. Share this with her."
- Make the share easy: a specific DM word (CYSTER), a specific result they can reference ("my period came back", "my HbA1c dropped"), a specific mechanism they understand ("insulin resistance was addressed first")
- Frame referral as an act of expertise, not loyalty: she is not doing you a favour — she is helping a woman she knows avoid wasting more time and money on the wrong protocol

# The Premium Buyer Filter — Quick Reference

Before every piece of content is published, run it through this filter. If it fails any check, rewrite through the PRISM lens.

The right 500 women seeing this content is worth more than 50,000 wrong ones.

Every content decision is made in service of that principle.

| CONTENT FAILS IF IT... | CONTENT PASSES IF IT... |
|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Describes the client as broken, without agency, or in need of rescue | Describes her as ambitious, strategic, and invested |
| Opens with a symptom list she already knows | Opens with a mechanism or diagnosis she hasn't heard framed this way |
| Could have been posted by any PCOS tips account | Could only have been posted by a hormone-first specialist |
| Uses empathy announcements ("I see you", "you're not alone") | Demonstrates understanding through specificity and accuracy |
| Presents results as emotional wins without mechanism | Frames results through the protocol: what it treated, why it worked |
| Reassures her that investing is okay | Assumes she is a strategic investor and speaks accordingly |
| Uses mass-market CTAs (save this, comment SOFT LIFE) | Uses buyer-ready CTAs (DM CYSTER, DM PPP) |

| Performs for the algorithm | Speaks directly to one specific woman who is already mostly sold |
