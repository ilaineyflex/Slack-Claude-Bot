# Make Clients Want You — Dona White

*Core thesis: Stop using generic market language. Build a "Category of One" by owning the vocabulary your ideal client uses to understand their own problem.*

This is grounded in solid theory — it maps directly to positioning work by Al Ries, Jack Trout, and more recently Christopher Lochhead's "Play Bigger." The psychology here is real: when a buyer adopts your language to describe their problem, cognitive dissonance kicks in and they feel your solution is the only logical fit.

## Cross-Checked Action Steps for Your Content AI

*What's validated by marketing psychology is noted alongside each step.*

**1. Audit your current language for commodity words**

Go through your existing content and flag every generic term you use — "clarity," "confidence," "strategy," "alignment," "transformation." These are words your competitors also use. Every one of them is a comparison point that weakens your positioning.

*Validated by: Blue Ocean Strategy's "eliminate-reduce" audit; Cialdini's contrast principle — you cannot be preferred if you can be compared.*

**2. Name your client's pain with a proprietary diagnosis**

Don't just describe what your client feels. Give it a named condition that only you own. The name doesn't need to be clever; it needs to be specific and yours. Format: "[Your Brand] + [What It Diagnoses]."

*Validated by: The "endowment effect" in behavioral economics — once clients adopt your term, abandoning it feels like a loss. Named conditions convert significantly better than symptom descriptions.*

**3. Create content that teaches the diagnosis, not the solution**

For high-ticket buyers especially, the sale happens at the problem-definition stage, not the solution stage. Sophisticated buyers distrust solutions pitched before they trust the diagnosis. Write content that helps them understand what's wrong using your framework. The solution becomes obvious once they accept your diagnosis.

*Validated by: Challenger Sale research (CEB/Gartner) — top-performing sellers reframe problems, they don't just solve stated ones. High-ticket buyers respond most to insight that redefines their situation.*

**4. Sell the methodology's inevitability, not the deliverables**

Stop listing modules, templates, and bonuses. High-ticket buyers are buying certainty of outcome. Frame every offer around what is guaranteed to happen when they go through your system. "When you implement X, Y becomes inevitable."

*Validated by: Loss aversion + certainty effect (Kahneman) — premium buyers pay a premium for reduced uncertainty, not for more features.*

**5. Repeat your proprietary language consistently across all content**

Your content AI needs a defined glossary of your terms and must use them in every piece. Frequency of exposure creates familiarity; familiarity creates trust; trust creates adoption of the language. Once a prospect is thinking in your terms, switching to a competitor requires re-learning their language.

*Validated by: Mere exposure effect (Zajonc); practically demonstrated by Tony Robbins ("limiting beliefs"), Brené Brown ("vulnerability"), and Alex Hormozi ("Grand Slam Offer").*

**6. Address the "invisible competitor": inaction and free alternatives**

High-ticket buyers rarely lose to a cheaper competitor — they lose to doing nothing or DIY. Your content must make inaction feel more costly than your price. Frame the cost of staying in the current situation using your diagnostic language.

*Validated by: Status quo bias research — the default choice is always "keep things as they are." High-ticket closes require making the status quo feel more painful than the change.*

**7. Use content to disqualify bad-fit clients publicly**

Content that says "this is not for you if..." signals scarcity and exclusivity to the buyers you do want. High-status buyers do not want what everyone can have.

*Validated by: Reactance theory + scarcity principle (Cialdini) — exclusivity increases perceived value. Luxury goods research shows broad accessibility reduces willingness to pay at the premium tier.*
